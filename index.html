A thermodynamic idea prototype

Spent nuclear fuel continues releasing decay heat. This app asks whether an assumed heat source could drive a simple closed-loop Brayton cycle. It does not calculate heat from a particular waste inventory, predict fuel temperatures, or establish engineering feasibility.

All scenarios are hypothetical. Available heat and temperature are independent inputs here; an actual source couples them through fuel age, inventory, geometry, heat transfer, and cooling. The high-temperature case is a thought experiment, not a claim about usable stored-waste heat.

Heat recovery does not remove radioactivity or the need for isolation and continuous heat removal. No reactor, reprocessing, material-handling, or shielding design is included.

Simple, transparent physics

Steady-state ideal-gas model, initially approximating nitrogen. All numerical assumptions are editable above. No recuperator or pressure losses are modeled. Temperatures in equations are kelvin. Changing gas properties creates a hypothetical ideal gas, not a validated material selection.

T₁ = cooling sink + cold-side approach
T₃ = source − hot-side approach
a = (γ − 1) / γ
T₂ = T₁ [1 + (rᵃ − 1) / ηc]
T₄ = T₃ [1 − ηt (1 − r⁻ᵃ)]
ṁ = Qcaptured / [cₚ (T₃ − T₂)]
Pshaft = ṁ cₚ [(T₃ − T₄) − (T₂ − T₁)]
Pnet = ηgenerator Pshaft − faux Qcaptured

For negative shaft work, electrical input is Pshaft / ηgenerator. Percent inputs are converted to fractions in the equations. A case is suppressed if captured heat is zero, a temperature is at or below absolute zero, the heating margin is not met, or the cooler cannot reject heat. The heating margin is editable. Mass flow is unconstrained, so outputs are not equipment ratings. Changing cₚ changes the inferred gas flow; at fixed heat and temperatures it cancels out of the power calculation.
